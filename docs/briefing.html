Project Briefing

Biotech Desk

An automated research analyst for small-cap pharma — and an honest account of what it can and cannot yet prove.

Status: operational since 14 August 2026 · 59 names · Private repository · Author: svedbg

1. What it does

Every weekday evening, without supervision, the system reads the primary record for a watchlist of small-cap pharmaceutical companies — SEC filings, prices, insider transactions, short interest, clinical trial registries — decides whether anything has genuinely changed, writes a research report, and sends a phone notification only when something is worth acting on. Most evenings it sends nothing, which is the intended behaviour.

STAGE 1
Facts
Deterministic collection from primary sources. No estimation, no inference. Fails loudly rather than guessing.
STAGE 2
Arithmetic
Fixed rules produce indicators, tiers and vetoes. Same input always yields the same output.
STAGE 3
Judgement
A language model reasons over stage 2, researches the news, and writes the report. It can be argued with.
STAGE 4
Delivery
HTML email with the report attached; phone push only on a genuine state change.

The separation between stages 1–2 and stage 3 is the central design decision. A language model is excellent at reading a prospectus and deciding whether an offering was priced at a discount. It is unreliable at remembering a share count. So it is never permitted to be the source of a number: every figure in the report traces to a file fetched from a primary source, or it is reported as unknown.

2. The problem it solves

A conventional stock screener applied to this sector produces a list of names that have fallen a long way. That list is close to worthless, because in small-cap biotech most large drawdowns are correctly pricing a broken thesis. The stock is cheap because the trial failed, the FDA said no, or the company is about to issue shares at a discount to survive.

The system's core mechanism is therefore not a signal but a refusal. A veto layer blocks any buy indication sitting on top of:

ConditionWhy it invalidates a low price
Priced offering (424B)New shares are being sold at a discount right now; the price is falling for a mechanical reason
Listing deficiency (8-K item 3.01)The exchange has started a delisting clock
Auditor change / non-relianceThe financial statements themselves are in question
Any −25% session in 10 daysSomething happened. Oversold indicators are at their most seductive exactly when they are most wrong
Under 1.5 quarters of cashA dilutive raise is near-certain, and it will be priced below the market

A veto can only be overturned in the report by citing specific evidence. This has already worked in practice in both directions: an offering flagged at Cogent was correctly identified as an at-the-market programme rather than a discounted takedown and cleared, while two Outlook Therapeutics filings were confirmed as genuine priced dilution and upheld.

4. Why it is credible: the thresholds are measured

Most systems of this kind are built on numbers chosen because they are conventional — RSI below 30, price in the bottom quartile. Here every threshold was tested against the project's own stored history (29,077 ticker-days, no lookahead) before being adopted. Three conventional choices proved wrong:

RuleFiresEdge @20d@60dVerdict

The finding that governs the whole system

When alerts are scored against XBI — the biotech ETF anyone can buy — rather than against an average of all days in the universe, the apparent edge disappears. Oversold alerts returned −2.28% median excess at 60 sessions. Only those confirmed by capitulation volume stayed positive (+0.25% median, +7.08% mean).

The earlier "edge" was measured against a baseline nobody can purchase. Against the investable alternative it is not there. The system now encodes this: the actionable tier requires volume confirmation, and the reports state plainly that the technical trigger is timing for names already wanted on fundamentals — never a reason to buy on its own.

5. Gotchas and honest limitations

Nothing is validated forward yet

Every performance number above is retrospective. It carries survivorship bias — acquired and delisted names are absent from the history — was measured during a period when the sector rose 24%, and models no transaction costs, which are material on micro-caps with wide spreads. Treat it as a smoke test of the thresholds, not evidence of profitability.

The data is full of traps, and each one produced a wrong answer first

TrapWhat it did before being fixed
Cash ≠ liquidityCounting only cash understated one company's runway six-fold and nearly triggered a false financing veto on a company with 13 quarters of money
Companies abandon XBRL tagsReturned a six-year-old $24.9M cash figure for a company holding $816M
Filer data can be nonsenseOne company's own reported float implied 15,401% of float short
Cash-flow figures are cumulativeA one-off licensing payment read as a burn rate, implying a funding crisis at a cash-generative company
XBRL lags the filingFresh quarterly numbers exist in an earnings release weeks before they are machine-readable

All are now guarded, and the guards are covered by tests. The lesson generalises: implausible financial data is far more often a broken tag than a broken company, and the system now proves the difference before acting on it.

Silence is indistinguishable from failure

The system's normal output is "nothing to do". A week of silence because the market was quiet and a week of silence because the scheduler broke look identical. A separate heartbeat process therefore raises an alarm if no report appears for two weekdays.

It cannot handicap a binary

Faced with a company seven days from an FDA decision after a negative advisory vote, the system says so prominently and then declines to advise. It can establish what happened, what is at stake and what the balance sheet supports. It cannot tell you what a regulator will decide, and it does not pretend to.

The principal risk is behavioural, not technical

The position-size ceiling is 28% of allocated capital per name. Applied to signals that have not been validated forward, that is an aggressive bet on reasoning rather than evidence. The paper-trading log exists precisely so the question can be settled at the cost of patience instead of capital.

7. What is missing

Deliberately absent

  • Order execution. Every trade is placed by hand. The system advises; it never transacts.
  • Position and portfolio data. It does not know what is owned, so it cannot leak it.
  • More signals. Nine factor families are already in use and none is validated. Adding a tenth would build further on unmeasured ground.

Genuine gaps

  • Forward validation — the single binding constraint. Everything else is secondary until the scorecard has data.
  • Options and implied volatility. For binary events the options market prices the expected move directly. No free source is currently wired in.
  • Intraday and pre-market coverage. Biotech news breaks before the open; the evening run sees only settled closes.
  • Foreign filers. Australian, Swiss and French issuers file no US-GAAP XBRL, so runway and float are simply unknown for them — correctly reported as unknown, but a real blind spot.
  • Commercial modelling. For approved drugs, reimbursement, payer coverage and launch trajectory drive returns. None is modelled.
  • Correlation-aware sizing. Aggregate exposure is summed and capped, but the names are highly correlated and a simple sum understates true concentration.

The zero-dependency constraint is a deliberate operational choice. An unattended job that runs for months should not be able to break because an unrelated package was upgraded elsewhere on the machine. A test enforces it: introducing a third-party import fails the build.

In one sentence

A disciplined, zero-cost research analyst that reads the primary record every evening, refuses to mistake a falling knife for a bargain, and — unusually — is built to tell its owner if it turns out not to work.

Research support for the owner's own decisions. Not financial advice; no orders are placed. Small-cap biotechnology carries near-binary outcomes and total loss of capital is a realistic outcome for any individual position.
